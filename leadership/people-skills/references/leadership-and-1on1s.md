# Leadership and 1-1s

Assumes the base frameworks in SKILL.md (Carnegie, OCEAN, Radical Candor) are already applied.

## 1-1 structure

- Their agenda first, always. A 1-1 that opens with the manager's status-check items trains the report to bring nothing.
- One coaching question per topic before advice: "what have you tried", "what's the constraint as you see it" - advice given before the report has stated their own read of the problem is usually wasted, and it deprives them of the rep.
- Close every 1-1 with a stated next action and owner, even informal ones - vagueness here is the most common reason 1-1s feel like they "don't go anywhere."

## Delegation vs. directing

- Match to the person's competence AND commitment on *this specific task*, not their general seniority (Situational Leadership). A senior person new to a task needs directing, not autonomy; a junior person on a task they've done ten times needs the delegation, not the checklist.
- Directing without explaining the why reads as micromanagement even when the call is correct - Carnegie's "talk in terms of the other person's interest" applies directly: state what's in it for them to do it your way, not just that it's the right way.

## Motivating vs. directing

- Autonomy, mastery, purpose (Drive, Pink) generally beats carrot/stick for knowledge work, but don't over-apply it to someone who is explicitly asking for clearer direction - that itself is a signal on the OCEAN Conscientiousness axis, not a motivation failure to fix.
- Public credit, private correction is a Carnegie-derived default, not an absolute - some people (low Neuroticism, high Extraversion) prefer direct public feedback and read the private-correction default as evasive. Check, don't assume.

## Team-level (not 1-1) leadership signals worth naming to the user

- Silence in a group setting is not agreement; it is frequently Ruinous Empathy running in reverse (the team protecting the leader, or each other, from a hard truth).
- A team that only escalates once a decision is already made past the point of influencing it is a Radical Candor failure at the team level, not just an individual one - worth naming as a pattern, not a one-off.
