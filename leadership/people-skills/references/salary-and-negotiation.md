# Salary and negotiation

Assumes the base frameworks in SKILL.md (Carnegie, OCEAN, Radical Candor) are already applied. If this is negotiation for a new role rather than a raise, also check whether `interview-coach` has already tailored context on the target role - reuse it instead of re-deriving.

## Before the conversation

- **BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement).** Know the walk-away option concretely before opening - a specific competing offer or a credible plan to stay/leave, not a vague sense of "I could probably find something else." A weak or unstated BATNA is the single biggest source of a weak negotiating position, more than the ask itself.
- **Anchoring.** The first concrete number stated tends to gravitate the rest of the conversation toward it. If you have a strong case, anchor first and slightly above target. If the other side anchors first with a low number, don't counter from their anchor - restate your own number and the evidence behind it rather than negotiating a middle from their starting point.
- Build the case in terms of value delivered and market data, not personal need ("rent went up") - the latter invites sympathy, not budget movement, and reads as a Carnegie-style appeal to the wrong motive for this specific ask.

## In the conversation

- Silence after stating a number is a tool, not a gap to fill - the instinct to soften a number by immediately justifying or discounting it is a Ruinous Empathy pattern showing up in a negotiation and it costs money.
- Separate comp components (base, bonus, equity, title, scope) so a "no" on one axis doesn't end the conversation - there is often room on a different axis even when the headline number is fixed.
- If the answer is no, ask what would need to be true for it to be yes next cycle, and get it in writing/documented somewhere it can be checked later - an undocumented "maybe next time" rarely resurfaces on its own.

## Reading the other side (OCEAN in this context)

- A counterpart high on Conscientiousness and process-orientation responds better to a written case ahead of the conversation than to being surprised with the ask live.
- A counterpart low on Agreeableness will not be moved by "it wouldn't be fair" framing - move to market data and retention risk framing instead.
